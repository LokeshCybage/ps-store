# Cricket rulebook

1. An over consists of six legal deliveries bowled by the same bowler from one end of the pitch.
2. A batsman is out bowled if a legal delivery dislodges both bails from the stumps at their end, or breaks the wicket, with the ball in play before the batsman has left their ground.
3. A batsman is out caught if a ball in play from a legal delivery touches the bat (or glove holding the bat) and is held by a fielder as a fair catch before the ball has touched the ground.
4. A batsman is out run out if a fielder breaks the wicket at the appropriate crease with the ball in play while the batsman is out of their ground and no other mode of dismissal applies.
5. A batsman is out leg before wicket if a legal delivery would have hit the wicket but for striking the batsman's body or protective equipment (not the bat or the glove holding the bat), with the ball pitching in line with the wickets or on the off side and the batsman offering no stroke or missing the ball with the bat.
6. A batsman is out stumped if the wicket-keeper breaks the wicket at the striker's end with the ball in play while the striker is out of their ground and has not attempted a run, following a delivery that is not a no-ball.
7. A batsman is out hit wicket if, while playing the ball or setting off for the first run, the striker breaks the wicket with the bat or any part of their clothing or equipment (other than the bat in a normal stroke), with the ball in play.
8. A batsman is out obstructing the field if, with the ball in play, they wilfully obstruct or distract any fielder by word or action when not legitimately defending their wicket or making a stroke at the ball.
9. A wide is called when a legal delivery passes wide of the striker where they are standing and would also have passed wide of them in a normal guard position, without having touched the bat or any part of the striker's person; one run is credited to the batting side and the delivery does not count as one of the six balls of the over unless the over is otherwise completed with wides or no-balls.
10. A no-ball is called if the bowler's front foot lands wholly beyond the popping crease before releasing the ball, if a full toss passes above the striker's waist in their normal stance (subject to exceptions for slow bowling in the laws), or for other specified illegal deliveries; one run is credited to the batting side and the delivery does not count as one of the six balls of the over.
11. After a no-ball, the next delivery is a free hit from which the striker may be dismissed only by run out, obstructing the field, or hit wicket.
12. A batsman is out handled the ball if, with the ball in play, they wilfully touch it with a hand not holding the bat without the consent of a fielder.
